### **Free cash flow exceeds target – Private banking activities expanded – Authorization requested for nominal value repayment and tenth dividend increase**

123 has set itself a long-term financial goal: it aims to achieve free cash flow amounting to CHF 800 million to 1 billion within ten years (from 2003 until 2012).

After four years, added value with a total of CHF 513.2 million is beyond the target range. Focusing on two core businesses and governance that aims for a bank with a strong balance sheet structure, robust refinancing, optimized earning power and a stronger equity position have once again proven to be the right strategy. Annual profit prior to taxes and prior to profit from the partial sale of a participation increased again in the past year for the fourteenth time in a row: in 2006 profits were up 4.3 percent to CHF 126.4 million. In addition, the bank registered an extraordinary after-tax profit amounting to CHF 42.5 million resulting from the sale of shares in BKW FMB Energie AG, which was allocated in full to reserves for general banking risks. Annual net profit (after deducting tax expenses amounting to CHF 46.6 million) was CHF 92.2 million, up CHF 7.1 million over the prior year.

### **Robust earning power**

123’s operational income was divided as follows in 2006: profit on interest: 56.2 percent; interest and dividends on financial assets: 6 percent; commissions and services: 21.9 percent; miscellaneous: 15.9 percent. Profit on interest amounted to CHF 278.8 million, a 2-percent increase relative to the prior year. Thanks to the loan policies that the bank has been applying consistently since their inception in 1993, dynamic loan processing and low interest rates, at-risk income again remained at a low level in 2006 (CHF 1.3 million).

123’s proven risk policy has enabled it to reduce the formation of new provisions for loans by CHF 0.4 million to CHF 5.1 million. Its asset management activities constitute its main source of commission and service income. The CHF 96.2 million in commissions the bank earned in 2006 was up 15.2 percent over 2005. The bank’s 2006 profit on commercial transactions (CHF 22.6 million) was on a par with that of the prior year, and was deliberately kept at a low level in accordance with the bank’s governance policies. Operating expenses rose by 1.2 percent to CHF 221.0 million subject to higher personnel expenses. The cost income ratio lay within the target range of 50 to 55 percent.

### **8014 new loans**

123 granted 8014 new loans worth a total of CHF 1.5 billion in 2006. Total borrowings (excluding public institutions) increased to CHF 13.9 billion, and total mortgage obligations rose by 3 percent to CHF 13.2 billion. The bank’s loan portfolio remained at the same high quality level as in the previous year. Non-performing loans accounted for less than 1 percent of all loans.

The share of individual loans accounted for by mortgages, fixed disbursements and credits worth less than CHF 1 million rose to 89 percent, whereas loans exceeding CHF 30 million accounted for less than 5 percent of all loans. In view of the fact that interest rates increased moderately, more variable interest mortgages were concluded while demand for fixed interest mortgages declined slightly, accounting for 75.1 percent of all mortgages as at the end of 2006 (versus 77.7 percent in 2005). In granting loans, the bank checks to ensure that customers have the financial capability to repay loans even if interest rates stabilize at a higher level. Its provisions for individual and lump sum reimbursements remained unchanged as at the end of the year (4.3 percent of all customer loans).

### **Private banking activities expanded**

123 expanded both the private banking and institutional investor domains in 2006. The number of investment accounts increased by 2.8 percent to 86,900, while the deposit value of managed customer assets rose by 1.2 billion to CHF 20.1 billion, a 6.3 percent increase over 2005. The value of discretionary portfolios (assets entrusted to the Bank for management) rose by CHF 0.3 billion to CHF 4.8 billion, accounting for approximately 25 percent of all assets on deposit. Demand for investment funds remained high. Funds in customer deposits rose in 2006 by CHF 0.2 billion to CHF 3.3 billion.

These products provide 100 percent capital protection per maturity, thus also making them an attractive alternative to conventional fixed interest deposits. The need for comprehensive advice concerning financial, tax and pension planning, the succession process and long-term savings was met by the financial consulting team. The bank’s over the counter transaction activities grew in 2006. Since late 2005, the Valiant Group has been realizing their over the counter stocks transactions of non-listed companies via 123’s electronic trading platform. These value-added services for SMEs have optimized transparency for over the counter stock transactions. The shares of approximately 335 SMEs were traded via this platform in 2006.

### **Outstanding equity position**

Pursuant to economic principles, 123 divides its stockholders’ equity into three categories: Target stockholders’ equity of CHF 1 billion, reserves for general banking risks of actually CHF 536 million and surplus capital of CHF 85 million. The reserves for general banking risks are required fluctuation reserves and can be increased or used in accordance with the risk exposure situation. Inasmuch as these funds are earmarked for specific purposes, they do not count as equity from an operational standpoint. 123 equity as at the end of the reporting year amounted to CHF 1.12 billion, which far exceeds the statutory requirement.

123 adheres to an equity strategy entailing a low-risk profile and robust equity holdings. An additional CHF 100 million was allocated to reserves for general banking risks in 2006. During the reporting year, CHF 15.8 million of the reserves for general banking risks were used for the following purposes: payment of the state guarantee, payment for services that allow for the reduction of migration risk, and for the reduction of interest fluctuation risk.

### **Long-term financial objective**

123 has set itself a long-term financial objective: it aims to achieve free cash flow amounting to CHF 800 million to 1 billion within ten years (from 2003 until 2012).

This will entail generating the equivalent of the bank’s target equity, which is some 120 percent of the required equity amount. The cumulative free cash flow of CHF 513.2 million in value added between 2003 and 2006 exceeds the original target. Of the free cash flow that will be achieved by 2012, half will be disbursed to share owners and half will be used for business development purposes. The value added in individual years may vary considerably according to the state of the economy and the amount of investments realized.

123’s primary goal has always been to provide the citizens of the canton of Berne with a robust and sound bank. 123 is committed to the realization of efficient bank governance accompanied by expert oversight so that it can do its part to further the development of the canton of Berne and its infrastructures. 123 bases all of its activities on proximity to its clientele; a broad stockholder and customer base; and the maintenance and development of a work force that is committed to continuity. For continuity is what 123 policies and management are all about, and it intends to continue pursuing this goal in the future.

### **Corporate governance**

The following principles apply to corporate governance:

- 1) Separation of Board of Directors and Executive Board.

- 2) No appointment of members of the Executive Board to the board of directors of companies where a member of 123’s Board of Directors sits on the executive board.

- 3) The compensation model for members of the Board of Directors is set by the entire Board of Directors and published in full.

- 4) The Executive Board’s remuneration is determined by the Board of Director’s Committee to which no members of the Executive Board belong. The highest overall compensation equals a maximum of a twenty fold of the lowest overall compensation.

- 5) 123 does not pay any leaving remunerations.

- 6) No further payments as defined in par. 5.7 of the Corporate Governance Guidelines (RLCG of 1.7.2002 issued by SWX Swiss Exchange), such as consultancy fees, are paid in addition to the payments made under the Board of Directors’ remuneration model.
